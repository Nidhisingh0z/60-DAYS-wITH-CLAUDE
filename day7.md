My Claude Usage Strategy
Recommended Primary Model
Claude Sonnet 5 — your daily driver.
Why This Model Fits Me
You're not running high-volume workloads, and your tasks (learning concepts, writing/debugging code, polishing a resume, researching topics) are exactly what Sonnet is built for. It's strong enough for real coding and analytical work, fast enough for back-and-forth learning conversations, and — since you're an occasional user — you won't come close to straining any usage limits. Save Opus for the few moments each week that actually need extra depth, rather than making it your default.
When to Use Haiku

Quick factual lookups ("what does this error mean?")
Summarizing a short article or a page of notes
Fast syntax checks, simple formatting/rewriting
Anytime you just need a fast answer, not deep thinking

When to Use Sonnet

Your everyday default for almost everything
Coding: writing scripts, debugging, explaining code
Learning support: concept explanations, study guides, practice problems
Resume/career prep: drafting, editing, tailoring to job descriptions
Standard research: summarizing papers, comparing sources, structuring an essay

When to Use Opus

Genuinely hard problems: a tricky algorithm/bug Sonnet couldn't solve
Deep, multi-source research synthesis for a thesis, dissertation, or major project
High-stakes deliverables: a job/internship application, a competitive scholarship essay, a final capstone report
Complex reasoning tasks where you need Claude to plan multiple steps and self-check its own work

Recommended Effort Level
Low
Quick lookups, simple rewrites, casual questions — pairs with Haiku.
Standard
Your default for most work — coding help, learning explanations, first drafts, everyday research. Best balance of quality and speed.
High
Use when a Standard-effort answer feels shallow or when the task genuinely needs careful reasoning: debugging a subtle bug, working through a hard concept step-by-step, structuring a deep research summary.
Max
Reserve for your hardest, highest-stakes moments only: a make-or-break project, a complex research synthesis across many sources, or a problem where Sonnet at High effort already struggled and you're escalating to Opus.

Biggest Mistakes I Should Avoid

Defaulting to Opus for everything — wastes time and usage headroom on tasks Sonnet handles just as well.
Using Max effort out of habit — it's slower and only worth it for genuinely hard problems.
Using Haiku for real learning/coding — it's great for speed, but you'll get shallower explanations than you need for actual understanding.
Not escalating when stuck — if Sonnet gives a weak answer twice on the same hard problem, switch to Opus instead of retrying the same model.
Treating research like a single prompt — for deep research, build it up in stages (outline → gather → synthesize) rather than expecting one perfect answer.

Final Recommendation
If you could use only ONE model and ONE effort level, go with:

Claude Sonnet 5 at Standard effort.

It comfortably covers coding, learning, research, and resume work at a quality level that will satisfy nearly everything a student needs — without wasting time or usage limit on tasks that don't require it. Keep Opus in your back pocket for the handful of high-stakes or genuinely hard moments (a tough bug, a critical application, a deep research synthesis) rather than using it as your everyday tool.
